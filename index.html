Lifestyle begins with the gospel

In the chapters that follow, you will explore practical and sometimes sensitive areas of Christian life. Before you consider any of them, hold firmly to this truth: changing your behaviour is never a way to earn God’s favour.

Big idea

You are saved only through Christ. Yet the Christ who forgives you also awakens love, reshapes your desires and leads you into a life that increasingly reflects His character.

A necessary distinction:

Biblical lifestyle instruction is not a ladder by which you climb into salvation. It is guidance for you after you have already been met by the grace of Jesus.

Behaviour cannot merit salvation

Christ alone is the basis of forgiveness

Hold firmly to this truth: nothing you do, and nothing you abstain from doing, can purchase favour with God. Your salvation rests on the merits of Christ’s life and sacrifice.

What behaviour cannot do

It cannot atone for sin

Effort, zeal, tears and self-denial cannot become a substitute for the blood of Jesus. Human conduct is never the ground of acceptance with God.

What salvation produces

It awakens love and holiness

When Christ’s sacrifice is received by faith, love for Him springs up in the heart and gives birth to the pursuit of Christlikeness.

Rules or relationship?

Love and behaviour are not rivals

Do not place relationship and obedience in opposition. In every healthy relationship, love naturally affects conduct. The same is true in your walk with Jesus.

An everyday illustration

Love changes how a spouse behaves

A husband who wants a stronger marriage stops actions that create conflict and does more of what blesses his wife. His changed behaviour makes love visible.

The spiritual application

Love for Christ changes how you live

When the Holy Spirit shows you that a behaviour is separating you from Christ, repentance and change express your love, trust and desire for deeper fellowship.

Behaviour without relationship

Can become cold formalism: outward correctness without living love for Christ.

Christ-centred obedience

Love receives Jesus, trusts His wisdom and submits practical life to His will.

Relationship talk without behaviour

Can become a way of protecting cherished sin while claiming closeness to Christ.

Keep the order clear:

Your obedience does not create the relationship or earn salvation. It protects and deepens your fellowship with Christ as you respond to His love.

“Is this a salvation issue?”

The question beneath the question

Scripture recognises that some sins carry greater weight than others. Yet you should never use that truth to dismiss any clear biblical instruction as unimportant.

A lowest-standard question
“How far can I digress and yet be saved?”
→
A love-shaped question
“How can I please God and more fully conform to His will?”

Consider three biblical examples. In each case, the outward act may appear small, but it reveals a deeper issue of trust and submission.

The outward action may appear slight. The deeper reality was choosing another voice over God’s clear command.

Cain modified what God had asked him to bring. The issue was not creative preference, but resistance to divine instruction.

Reaching out to steady the ark appeared helpful, but it disregarded God’s explicit instructions concerning sacred things.

Watch the rationalisation:

The habits you call “little things” may be the very things with the tightest grip on you. Choose honest surrender rather than minimum compliance.

God’s purpose in practical counsel

God is not trying to take joy away

When Scripture confronts one of your habits or choices, surrender can feel like loss. See God’s instruction for what it is: the counsel of a wise Creator who has your well-being in view.

Reframe the command

God’s practical counsel is not arbitrary restriction. Receive it as mercy expressed through guidance, protection and an invitation into something better.

Practical application

Come to Jesus honestly, not hopelessly

Your struggle with practical obedience is not a reason to despair. Come to the sympathetic and forgiving Saviour. He receives you as you are, cleanses you and leads you forward.
